Overview

Nothing is critical if everything is critical.

India's National Critical Mineral Mission commits ₹34,300 crore over seven years to secure a list of 51 minerals. But of the roughly 76 elements the world mines for commercial use, India designates 51 as critical, two-thirds of the usable periodic table. A label applied that broadly cannot tell the Mission where the next rupee should go. If everything is a priority, nothing is.

The deeper problem is that "critical" is a single label stretched over very different problems. It puts copper in the same category as ytterbium, a base metal mined by the megatonne sitting beside a trace rare earth refined almost entirely in one country. The two share almost nothing about how their supply is secured. A mineral can be hard to secure because it is geologically scarce, because its refining sits in one country, because it has no substitute, or because its price is weaponised. Each calls for a different response. In the scramble to diversify away from China's dominance, that distinction is the first thing lost.

India holds large reserves but almost none of the mining and processing capacity to turn them into usable supply. Closing that gap on a fixed budget means knowing not just what is important, but why, where the bottleneck sits, and how to fix it. This dashboard takes the list apart: it scores all 51 minerals across ten measurable vectors and reads the shape of each profile, never a single total, to sort them into six groups that each warrant a distinct response. Defining criticality properly is what tells the state where to act, and where it need not act at all.

Methodology

How We Score Criticality

Ten measurable vectors, each capturing one dimension of criticality. Eight are tied to published quantitative thresholds. No composite score is produced.

The framework's value rests on its scores being contestable rather than impressionistic.

Understanding the Six Groups

The minerals do not fall into neatly separated natural clusters — they occupy a continuous space. Clustering imposes boundaries on that spectrum rather than discovering gaps in it. The boundaries are nonetheless informative.
