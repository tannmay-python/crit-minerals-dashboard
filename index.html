The Analytical Foundation

Nothing is critical
if everything is critical.

Criticality lists have proliferated across governments, multilaterals, and research institutions — each more expansive than the last. India's own critical minerals list identifies 30 commodities; count the 17 rare earth elements individually and the tally reaches 47. At that scale, the label "critical" stops meaning anything. Policy attention, diplomatic capital, and strategic investment cannot be distributed equally across four dozen minerals — scarcity of focus is itself a strategic vulnerability.

Not all critical minerals are critical in the same way. Terbium — with global demand of roughly 400–600 tonnes per year, a near-monopoly Chinese supply chain, and applications confined to specialist magnetic alloys — is critical through scarcity and geopolitical chokehold. Potassium — consumed in hundreds of millions of tonnes, with a diversified producer base across Canada, Russia, and Belarus — is critical through sheer volume and food-system dependency. The policy response these two minerals require is entirely different: one demands access diplomacy and substitution research; the other demands supply diversification and strategic stockpiling.

A one-size-fits-all approach to critical minerals is not good policy design. Understanding what drives criticality for each mineral — whether it is supply concentration, demand trajectory, substitutability, India's specific import exposure, or price weaponization risk — is the analytical foundation for effective strategy. That is what this dashboard is built to provide: a framework for disaggregating criticality, so that the right interventions can be targeted at the right minerals.
