Critical Minerals Analysis · India

"Critical mineral lists have expanded to the point that they are no longer effective policy tools."

India's official critical minerals list has 30 entries — but two of them, rare earth elements and platinum group elements, are groups of 17 and 6 elements respectively. Fully expanded, the Indian list covers 51 elements: about two-thirds of everything mined for commercial use. If two-thirds of the periodic table is "critical," the designation has ceased to mean anything.

The stakes are not abstract. In 2025, China placed rare earths under export licensing and Indian automotive and electronics manufacturers were initially denied licenses, facing sudden supply disruption. India is acutely exposed: it holds large mineral reserves but lacks almost any of the mining or processing capacity to translate them into usable supply. The National Critical Mineral Mission has committed ₹34,300 crore over seven years — but a programme that must spread a finite budget across 51 entries with no embedded data distinguishing one mineral from another has no way to prioritise where the next rupee should go.

The root problem is structural. "Criticality" is treated as a single binary condition when it is the result of several constituent factors. A mineral can be hard to secure because it is geologically scarce, because its refining sits in one country, because it has no substitute, or because its price is weaponised. Each of these calls for a different response — a flat list cannot tell you which.

This dashboard accompanies a framework that decomposes criticality into ten measurable vectors, scores all 51 minerals against defined criteria, and uses the shape of each mineral's profile to sort them into six groups. Minerals with similar profiles suffer from similar bottlenecks and warrant similar interventions. The scored dataset is offered as an open resource: because every vector is recorded separately, anyone can reweight the dimensions for their own purpose, test relationships, or extend it to further minerals.

Methodology

Understanding the Six Groups

The minerals do not fall into neatly separated natural clusters — they occupy a continuous space. Clustering imposes boundaries on that spectrum rather than discovering gaps in it. The boundaries are nonetheless informative.
